# Education direction and Mural feature review

Research date: October 23, 2026. This is a proposed direction, not a list of newly implemented features or validated learning outcomes.

## Product purpose

Help educators and facilitators prepare, run, and improve learning activities while learners understand the task, contribute their thinking, and act on feedback.

The intended scope includes K–12, higher education, adult learning, and workplace training. A shared workflow, **prepare → facilitate → participate → reflect**, can serve each setting with appropriate language, scaffolding, examples, and participation options. Keep short independent activities and the existing classroom Homebase useful within this broader direction.

## What the research establishes

Mural's public materials describe visual workspaces, facilitation, participation, templates, integrations, and AI organization/synthesis. Its [teaching course](https://learn.mural.co/courses/teaching-with-mural) already applies the platform to education. Boardwork's opportunity is to make learning-specific workflows and educator judgment easier to support.

This review uses official public documentation and the previously inspected public template gallery. It is not a hands-on verification of paid features or plan eligibility. The old `/education` URL redirected to Mural's homepage during research. Teaching materials inform use cases; current product pages support the feature inventory. The proposed adaptations below require user research and testing.

## One product with adaptable guidance

Age and setting are starting points. Prior knowledge, reading fluency, language, access needs, and task complexity should remain separate considerations. Adult language learners, for example, need adult-relevant content with suitable language support.

| Setting | Suggested starting experience | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Early primary | Educator-led display, visual/spoken directions, one action at a time, physical or drawn responses. | Sort objects and explain one choice. |
| Upper primary | Short stages, worked examples, simple organizers, and scaffolded explanation. | Predict, observe, and explain a change. |
| Secondary | Evidence, comparison, design decisions, critique, and increasing independence. | Compare prototypes against user needs and justify a revision. |
| Higher education | Case analysis, seminar preparation, research synthesis, and collaborative models. | Map competing explanations and the evidence distinguishing them. |
| Adult/workplace learning | Practical scenarios, relevant experience, rehearsal, feedback, and application. | Practise a customer conversation and choose an action to apply at work. |

These are defaults to test, not assumptions about every learner. Use context-appropriate labels such as class, course, cohort, or workshop. A shared activity model should support them. This document does not change existing UI labels or generation constraints.

## Delivery and participation

Start with the existing projected/device-off experience: educators facilitate while learners use paper, materials, or discussion. Shared devices, individual devices, and asynchronous participation are additional modes with their own access and saving requirements. A template alone does not create a shared learning session.

## Proposed sequence

1. **Dependable content and reusable facilitation:** build the AI quality rubric and a held-out regression set; add curated templates, Save as template, ordered stages, deliberate reveal, and optional stage timing. Test with school educators and adult-learning facilitators.
2. **Richer visual learning:** add structured organizers, relationship tools, collections, and printable/exportable outcomes where research supports the need.
3. **Shared learning sessions:** introduce educator ownership, isolated cohorts, scoped participant access, visibility rules, moderation, retention, and conflict-safe editing. Then add submissions, peer feedback, private think-and-reveal, and asynchronous work.
4. **Synthesis and wider integration:** evaluate educator-reviewed response summaries, authenticated resource connections, and agent workflows after the supporting data and access model exist.

An initial template set could include Notice and Predict, Sort and Explain, Compare Evidence, Generate Alternatives, Understand a User, Practise a Scenario, Peer Critique, and Reflect and Apply. Provide original examples and facilitator guidance appropriate to each audience.

## Validation

Compare with educators' current practice. Observe preparation time, editing required, ability to begin independently, repeat use, recovery from mistakes, and willingness to pay. Examine the quality of explanations and application to realistic tasks; activity completion alone does not establish learning.

For AI, evaluate correctness, first-action clarity, audience fit, learning demand, time/material limits, language support, and sparse/rich context. Response synthesis needs checks for distortion and omitted ideas. Across ages, validate projected readability, accessibility, and the amount of additional explanation required.
